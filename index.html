Five Roads to the Legion of Honor

A bronze is cast from a mold, so it can be made again and again, even after the artist has died. All five of these bronzes at the Legion of Honor came out of the same foundry in Paris: two in Rodin's lifetime, two after his death, and one from a plaster by the California sculptor Arthur Putnam, cast because Alma de Bretteville Spreckels paid for it. Walter Benjamin argued that copies lose the "aura" of an original. Follow each work from the foundry to San Francisco to see how the art market, two world wars and the Depression shaped where these copies went, and whether they lost anything on the way.
